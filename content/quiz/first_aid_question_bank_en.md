## CPR

### 1. An adult collapses, does not respond, and only gasps. What should you do?

- A. Call emergency services, get an AED, and start CPR
- B. Give water and wait
- C. Put the person on their feet
- D. Place a pillow under their head

**Correct answer:** A. Call emergency services, get an AED, and start CPR

**Explanation:** Gasping is not normal breathing. Activate emergency help and begin CPR.

### 2. What does CPR stand for?

- A. Cardiac Pressure Recovery
- B. Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation
- C. Circulation Pulse Repair
- D. Critical Patient Response

**Correct answer:** B. Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation

**Explanation:** CPR means cardiopulmonary resuscitation.

### 3. Where should you place your hands for adult chest compressions?

- A. On the upper abdomen
- B. Over the left ribs
- C. In the center of the chest on the lower half of the breastbone
- D. At the base of the neck

**Correct answer:** C. In the center of the chest on the lower half of the breastbone

**Explanation:** Use the lower half of the sternum, in the center of the chest.

### 4. What is the recommended adult chest-compression rate?

- A. 40–60 per minute
- B. 60–80 per minute
- C. 80–90 per minute
- D. 100–120 per minute

**Correct answer:** D. 100–120 per minute

**Explanation:** The recommended adult rate is 100–120 compressions per minute.

### 5. How deep should adult chest compressions generally be?

- A. About 1 cm
- B. At least 5 cm, avoiding depths over 6 cm
- C. About 2 cm
- D. As deep as possible

**Correct answer:** B. At least 5 cm, avoiding depths over 6 cm

**Explanation:** Aim for at least 5 cm and avoid excessive depth beyond 6 cm.

### 6. An AED arrives while you are doing CPR. What should you do?

- A. Ignore it until the ambulance arrives
- B. Use it only if the person wakes up
- C. Turn it on and follow its voice prompts
- D. Put the pads over clothing

**Correct answer:** C. Turn it on and follow its voice prompts

**Explanation:** Use an AED as soon as available and follow its prompts.

### 7. You are alone with a phone and an adult who is unresponsive and not breathing normally. What is best?

- A. Call emergency services on speakerphone and start CPR
- B. Leave for several minutes to find help
- C. Wait for another person
- D. Give the person a drink

**Correct answer:** A. Call emergency services on speakerphone and start CPR

**Explanation:** Speakerphone lets you summon help while beginning CPR.

### 8. When should rescuers switch the person doing compressions, if another rescuer is available?

- A. Every 10 minutes
- B. About every 2 minutes, with minimal interruption
- C. Only after 15 minutes
- D. Never switch

**Correct answer:** B. About every 2 minutes, with minimal interruption

**Explanation:** Switching about every two minutes can reduce fatigue and maintain quality.

### 9. You are not trained or willing to give rescue breaths to an adult in sudden cardiac arrest. What can you do?

- A. Do nothing
- B. Give abdominal thrusts
- C. Perform hands-only chest compressions and follow dispatcher instructions
- D. Place the person face down

**Correct answer:** C. Perform hands-only chest compressions and follow dispatcher instructions

**Explanation:** Hands-only CPR is appropriate for an untrained bystander responding to an adult collapse.

### 10. When may a lay rescuer stop CPR?

- A. As soon as help is called
- B. After exactly 2 minutes
- C. When tired for a few seconds
- D. When the person breathes normally, trained help takes over, the scene becomes unsafe, or the rescuer cannot continue

**Correct answer:** D. When the person breathes normally, trained help takes over, the scene becomes unsafe, or the rescuer cannot continue

**Explanation:** Continue until recovery signs, trained handover, danger, or inability to continue.

## Bleeding

### 1. A person's forearm is bleeding heavily from a deep cut. What should you do first?

- A. Apply firm, continuous direct pressure and call for help
- B. Wash it for ten minutes before pressing
- C. Let it bleed freely
- D. Apply a loose bandage only

**Correct answer:** A. Apply firm, continuous direct pressure and call for help

**Explanation:** Firm, continuous pressure is the first action for severe external bleeding.

### 2. Blood soaks through the dressing. What should you do?

- A. Remove all dressings and start over
- B. Add more material on top and keep pressing
- C. Stop pressure to inspect the wound
- D. Wash the wound again

**Correct answer:** B. Add more material on top and keep pressing

**Explanation:** Maintain pressure and add more dressing on top rather than repeatedly removing the first layer.

### 3. A knife is embedded in someone's leg. What should you do?

- A. Pull it out quickly
- B. Push it deeper to stop movement
- C. Apply pressure directly onto the object
- D. Do not remove it; apply pressure around it and get emergency help

**Correct answer:** D. Do not remove it; apply pressure around it and get emergency help

**Explanation:** Removing an embedded object may worsen bleeding. Apply pressure around it.

### 4. Which may indicate life-threatening bleeding?

- A. A tiny paper cut
- B. A small bruise
- C. Blood spurting or rapidly pooling
- D. A healed scar

**Correct answer:** C. Blood spurting or rapidly pooling

**Explanation:** Spurting or rapidly pooling blood may indicate severe bleeding.

### 5. What should you do after putting pressure on a bleeding wound?

- A. Keep pressure continuous until bleeding is controlled or help takes over
- B. Check it every five seconds by lifting the dressing
- C. Ask the person to exercise
- D. Leave the person alone

**Correct answer:** A. Keep pressure continuous until bleeding is controlled or help takes over

**Explanation:** Repeatedly lifting the dressing can disrupt clot formation.

### 6. When can a tourniquet be appropriate?

- A. For every small cut
- B. For life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg, if available and you know how or are instructed
- C. For nosebleeds
- D. For bruises

**Correct answer:** B. For life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg, if available and you know how or are instructed

**Explanation:** A tourniquet can control life-threatening limb bleeding; follow training and emergency instructions.

### 7. What is a sensible way to protect yourself while treating blood loss?

- A. Touch blood with bare hands whenever possible
- B. Use a shared cloth for every casualty
- C. Avoid all help even if safe gloves are available
- D. Use gloves or another barrier if available

**Correct answer:** D. Use gloves or another barrier if available

**Explanation:** Use gloves or a suitable barrier when available and wash hands afterward.

### 8. A person with severe bleeding becomes pale, clammy, and confused. What does this suggest?

- A. They are hungry
- B. They are embarrassed
- C. Possible shock; call emergency services and continue bleeding control
- D. The bleeding is definitely harmless

**Correct answer:** C. Possible shock; call emergency services and continue bleeding control

**Explanation:** Pale, clammy skin and confusion may be signs of shock.

### 9. What should you do if a wound has a small object stuck in it?

- A. Press around it and seek appropriate medical advice; do not dig it out
- B. Probe deeply with tweezers
- C. Pour household cleaner into it
- D. Ignore it regardless of depth

**Correct answer:** A. Press around it and seek appropriate medical advice; do not dig it out

**Explanation:** Avoid digging into wounds or removing embedded objects.

### 10. After bleeding is controlled, what is important?

- A. Hide the injury and continue activity
- B. Arrange medical assessment for deep, gaping, contaminated, or serious wounds
- C. Remove any clot with a cloth
- D. Apply butter

**Correct answer:** B. Arrange medical assessment for deep, gaping, contaminated, or serious wounds

**Explanation:** Deep or contaminated wounds may need professional treatment and tetanus assessment.

## Burns

### 1. Hot tea spills onto a person's hand. What is the best immediate action?

- A. Apply ice directly
- B. Rub butter onto it
- C. Cool under cool running water for 20 minutes
- D. Break any blisters

**Correct answer:** C. Cool under cool running water for 20 minutes

**Explanation:** Cool a thermal burn under cool running water for 20 minutes as soon as possible.

### 2. Which should not be applied to a fresh burn?

- A. Cool running water
- B. A clean non-fluffy covering after cooling
- C. Removal of nearby jewellery if not stuck
- D. Ice, butter, or oil

**Correct answer:** D. Ice, butter, or oil

**Explanation:** Ice, butter, and oils can worsen tissue injury or interfere with care.

### 3. Clothing is stuck to a burn. What should you do?

- A. Pull it off quickly
- B. Leave stuck fabric in place and get medical help as needed
- C. Scrub the skin
- D. Cut into the blister

**Correct answer:** B. Leave stuck fabric in place and get medical help as needed

**Explanation:** Do not pull away clothing stuck to burned skin.

### 4. Which burn needs urgent medical assessment?

- A. A large, deep, chemical, electrical, or airway-related burn
- B. A tiny superficial red patch only
- C. A healed old scar
- D. Mild redness with no pain or other concern

**Correct answer:** A. A large, deep, chemical, electrical, or airway-related burn

**Explanation:** Large, deep, chemical, electrical, facial, and airway burns can be serious.

### 5. What should you do with blisters caused by a burn?

- A. Pop them with a needle
- B. Rub them until they flatten
- C. Leave them intact
- D. Cover them with glue

**Correct answer:** C. Leave them intact

**Explanation:** Do not deliberately break blisters; protect the burn with a suitable clean covering.

### 6. A person has a chemical powder on their skin. What is a safe initial approach?

- A. Rub it into the skin
- B. Pour another chemical on it
- C. Cover it with oil
- D. Protect yourself and brush off dry powder before irrigation when appropriate; follow emergency guidance

**Correct answer:** D. Protect yourself and brush off dry powder before irrigation when appropriate; follow emergency guidance

**Explanation:** Dry chemicals may need to be brushed off before irrigating; avoid exposing yourself.

### 7. Why remove rings or a watch near a burn if they are not stuck?

- A. They cool the skin too much
- B. Swelling may make them difficult to remove later
- C. They cause blisters instantly
- D. They prevent pain

**Correct answer:** B. Swelling may make them difficult to remove later

**Explanation:** Burned areas can swell, so remove nearby jewellery early if it comes off easily.

### 8. What is a suitable way to cover a cooled burn?

- A. Loosely cover it with a clean, non-fluffy dressing
- B. Wrap it tightly with adhesive tape
- C. Apply cotton wool directly to the burn
- D. Cover it with soil

**Correct answer:** A. Loosely cover it with a clean, non-fluffy dressing

**Explanation:** A clean, non-fluffy covering helps protect the area without sticking.

### 9. A person receives an electrical burn. What should you do first?

- A. Touch them while current may still be flowing
- B. Pour water on live electrical equipment
- C. Ensure the electrical source is safe, call emergency help, and assess them
- D. Ask them to continue working

**Correct answer:** C. Ensure the electrical source is safe, call emergency help, and assess them

**Explanation:** Do not approach or touch the casualty until the electrical danger is controlled.

### 10. A burn victim develops hoarseness and difficulty breathing after a fire. What should you do?

- A. Offer food
- B. Wait for the skin to blister
- C. Apply cream to the throat
- D. Call emergency services immediately

**Correct answer:** D. Call emergency services immediately

**Explanation:** Airway symptoms after smoke or heat exposure may signal a life-threatening inhalation injury.

## Choking

### 1. A conscious adult cannot speak, breathe, or cough effectively while eating. What should you do?

- A. Give appropriate choking first aid and call for help
- B. Offer a glass of water
- C. Put fingers blindly into the throat
- D. Tell them to lie down and wait

**Correct answer:** A. Give appropriate choking first aid and call for help

**Explanation:** Severe choking requires immediate action; follow local first-aid guidance.

### 2. What is a sign of severe choking?

- A. Speaking clearly
- B. A strong, effective cough
- C. Inability to speak or breathe normally
- D. Sneezing once

**Correct answer:** C. Inability to speak or breathe normally

**Explanation:** A person with severe airway obstruction may be unable to speak, breathe, or cough effectively.

### 3. For a conscious adult with severe choking, what sequence is commonly recommended?

- A. Only give water
- B. Up to five back blows, then up to five abdominal thrusts; repeat and seek help
- C. Make them run
- D. Put them upside down

**Correct answer:** B. Up to five back blows, then up to five abdominal thrusts; repeat and seek help

**Explanation:** Follow local guidance: commonly alternate up to five back blows and five abdominal thrusts.

### 4. Why should you avoid blind finger sweeps?

- A. They always cure choking
- B. They help the object move deeper
- C. They are required for every choking case
- D. They may push an object farther into the airway

**Correct answer:** D. They may push an object farther into the airway

**Explanation:** Only remove an object if you can clearly see it and safely reach it.

### 5. A person can cough forcefully and speak while coughing. What should you encourage?

- A. Effective coughing while monitoring them
- B. Immediate blind finger sweep
- C. Abdominal thrusts regardless of severity
- D. Giving solid food

**Correct answer:** A. Effective coughing while monitoring them

**Explanation:** An effective cough may clear a partial obstruction; monitor for worsening.

### 6. A choking person becomes unresponsive. What should you do?

- A. Keep them standing
- B. Give water
- C. Lower them safely, call emergency services, and begin CPR as indicated
- D. Search their throat blindly

**Correct answer:** C. Lower them safely, call emergency services, and begin CPR as indicated

**Explanation:** If unresponsive, activate emergency help and start CPR; remove an object only if visible.

### 7. A baby under one year is severely choking. Which is appropriate?

- A. Abdominal thrusts
- B. Age-appropriate cycles of back blows and chest thrusts
- C. Hang the baby by the feet
- D. Sweep the mouth blindly

**Correct answer:** B. Age-appropriate cycles of back blows and chest thrusts

**Explanation:** Infants need a different technique: back blows and chest thrusts, not abdominal thrusts.

### 8. After a choking episode, when is medical assessment important?

- A. Never, regardless of symptoms
- B. Only if the person asks a week later
- C. Only if they are hungry
- D. If symptoms persist, thrusts were needed, or there is concern for injury or an object remaining

**Correct answer:** D. If symptoms persist, thrusts were needed, or there is concern for injury or an object remaining

**Explanation:** Persistent symptoms or forceful manoeuvres may warrant medical assessment.

### 9. What should you do if a person with mild choking can speak and breathe?

- A. Encourage coughing and monitor closely
- B. Immediately give abdominal thrusts to everyone
- C. Give a large drink
- D. Leave them alone

**Correct answer:** A. Encourage coughing and monitor closely

**Explanation:** Encourage coughing when it is effective and be ready to act if obstruction worsens.

### 10. Which action is unsafe during choking first aid?

- A. Calling for help
- B. Following emergency dispatcher instructions
- C. Offering food to push the obstruction down
- D. Monitoring the person's response

**Correct answer:** C. Offering food to push the obstruction down

**Explanation:** Do not offer food or drink to someone who is choking.

## Poisons

- A. Make them vomit
- B. Contact a poison centre or emergency service promptly and keep the container
- C. Give milk to neutralize it
- D. Wait until symptoms begin

**Correct answer:** B. Contact a poison centre or emergency service promptly and keep the container

**Explanation:** Some poisons cause delayed symptoms. Get specialist advice promptly.

### 2. Why should you not induce vomiting after a suspected poisoning?

- A. Vomiting always removes all poison
- B. It is needed only for children
- C. It makes every poison harmless
- D. It can cause further injury or aspiration

**Correct answer:** D. It can cause further injury or aspiration

**Explanation:** Vomiting may worsen injury or cause poison to enter the lungs.

### 3. A person has collapsed after inhaling fumes in an enclosed room. What should you do?

- A. Enter immediately without protection
- B. Light a match to check the air
- C. Stay out of danger, call emergency services, and follow hazardous-material guidance
- D. Open containers to identify the chemical

**Correct answer:** C. Stay out of danger, call emergency services, and follow hazardous-material guidance

**Explanation:** Do not become another casualty; avoid entering a contaminated atmosphere.

### 4. A poison splashes into someone's eye. What should you do?

- A. Immediately rinse with clean flowing water and seek poison/medical advice
- B. Rub the eye
- C. Cover it without rinsing
- D. Apply neutralizing chemicals

**Correct answer:** A. Immediately rinse with clean flowing water and seek poison/medical advice

**Explanation:** Prompt irrigation is important for many chemical eye exposures.

### 5. What information is useful when contacting a poison centre?

- A. Only the person's favourite food
- B. Substance name, estimated amount, time, and symptoms if known
- C. Only the weather
- D. Nothing; discard the package

**Correct answer:** B. Substance name, estimated amount, time, and symptoms if known

**Explanation:** Product and exposure details help specialists advise on the correct response.

### 6. An unconscious person may have swallowed poison. What should you avoid?

- A. Calling emergency services
- B. Monitoring breathing
- C. Following dispatcher instructions
- D. Giving anything by mouth

**Correct answer:** D. Giving anything by mouth

### 7. A chemical is spilled on skin. What should you generally do?

- A. Rub it vigorously
- B. Apply another chemical to neutralize it
- C. Protect yourself, remove contaminated clothing when safe, and rinse according to guidance
- D. Cover it with butter

**Correct answer:** C. Protect yourself, remove contaminated clothing when safe, and rinse according to guidance

**Explanation:** Avoid secondary exposure and follow the product-specific emergency guidance.

### 8. Which is a possible poisoning symptom?

- A. Confusion, vomiting, breathing difficulty, or seizures
- B. A clean fingernail
- C. Normal conversation only
- D. A healed scar

**Correct answer:** A. Confusion, vomiting, breathing difficulty, or seizures

**Explanation:** Poisoning symptoms vary and may include neurological, digestive, or breathing problems.

### 9. What should you do with the poison container?

- A. Throw it away immediately
- B. Keep it available for responders if safe
- C. Pour it into another unlabelled bottle
- D. Taste it to identify it

**Correct answer:** B. Keep it available for responders if safe

**Explanation:** Packaging or a photo of the label can help responders identify the substance.

### 10. A person has a seizure after suspected poisoning. What is the priority?

- A. Force a drink into their mouth
- B. Hold them down tightly
- C. Wait alone until it ends
- D. Call emergency services, protect them from injury, and put nothing in their mouth

**Correct answer:** D. Call emergency services, protect them from injury, and put nothing in their mouth

**Explanation:** Seizure and suspected poisoning require urgent assistance and basic seizure safety.

### 10. Which bite-related symptom is an emergency?

## Universal First Aid Algorithm

- A. Check scene safety and call emergency services if needed
- B. Run into traffic immediately
- C. Move every casualty at once
- D. Start treatment without looking around

**Correct answer:** A. Check scene safety and call emergency services if needed

**Explanation:** Rescuer and scene safety come first.

### 2. What is a sensible first assessment sequence for an adult casualty?

- A. Give food, then ask their name
- B. Check safety, response, and normal breathing; call for help and provide appropriate care
- C. Move the neck in every direction
- D. Wait silently for ten minutes

**Correct answer:** B. Check safety, response, and normal breathing; call for help and provide appropriate care

**Explanation:** A systematic assessment helps identify immediate life threats.

### 3. A responsive adult refuses help after a minor injury. What should you generally do?

- A. Force treatment on them
- B. Ignore their words
- C. Respect their decision if they have capacity, explain concerns, and seek help if danger is serious
- D. Give medicine secretly

**Correct answer:** C. Respect their decision if they have capacity, explain concerns, and seek help if danger is serious

**Explanation:** A competent adult can usually refuse care; explain and respect their choice.

### 4. When should you call emergency services?

- A. Only when there is visible blood
- B. Only if the casualty asks twice
- C. Never for an unconscious person
- D. For life-threatening symptoms such as unresponsiveness, abnormal breathing, severe bleeding, or stroke signs

**Correct answer:** D. For life-threatening symptoms such as unresponsiveness, abnormal breathing, severe bleeding, or stroke signs

**Explanation:** Recognize emergencies early and activate professional help.

### 5. Why use gloves or a barrier when possible?

- A. To reduce exposure to blood and body fluids
- B. To make the casualty sleepy
- C. To replace handwashing forever
- D. To avoid calling for help

**Correct answer:** A. To reduce exposure to blood and body fluids

### 6. A casualty is unconscious but breathing normally, and no major trauma is suspected. What may be appropriate?

- A. Give them water
- B. Leave them face down
- C. Place them in the recovery position and monitor breathing
- D. Make them stand

**Correct answer:** C. Place them in the recovery position and monitor breathing

### 7. What should you do if a casualty is not breathing normally?

- A. Put them in a chair
- B. Call emergency services, get an AED, and start CPR
- C. Give them a snack
- D. Wait for their colour to change

**Correct answer:** B. Call emergency services, get an AED, and start CPR

**Explanation:** Absent or abnormal breathing with unresponsiveness requires emergency action.

### 8. Why should you obtain consent before treating a responsive adult?

- A. It is optional in every situation
- B. It makes the injury worse
- C. It replaces first-aid skills
- D. It respects the person's autonomy

### 9. A scene becomes unsafe while you are helping. What should you do?

- A. Move to safety and call for professional assistance
- B. Stay regardless of danger
- C. Ask bystanders to ignore the hazard
- D. Turn your back on the hazard and continue

### 10. After giving first aid, what information should you tell responders?

- A. Your guess about the person's identity only
- B. Only your opinion of the injury
- C. What happened, what you observed, and what care you gave
- D. Nothing at all

**Correct answer:** C. What happened, what you observed, and what care you gave

**Explanation:** Clear, factual handover supports ongoing care.

## Fractures

### 1. A cyclist has a visibly deformed forearm after a fall. What should you do?

- A. Force it straight
- B. Support it in the position found and seek medical help
- C. Ask them to lift a heavy object
- D. Massage it firmly

**Correct answer:** B. Support it in the position found and seek medical help

**Explanation:** Do not attempt to straighten a suspected fracture.

### 2. Bone is visible through a wound. What should you do?

- A. Push the bone back in
- B. Wash it with household bleach
- C. Apply pressure directly to the exposed bone
- D. Cover the wound with a sterile dressing and seek emergency care; do not push bone in

**Correct answer:** D. Cover the wound with a sterile dressing and seek emergency care; do not push bone in

**Explanation:** An open fracture needs urgent care. Protect the wound without manipulating the bone.

### 3. Which can be a sign of fracture?

- A. Pain, swelling, bruising, deformity, or loss of function
- B. A normal painless movement
- C. A healed scar
- D. A mild sneeze

**Correct answer:** A. Pain, swelling, bruising, deformity, or loss of function

**Explanation:** Fractures may cause pain, swelling, bruising, deformity, and difficulty using the limb.

### 4. Why support a suspected fracture?

- A. To test how much it can move
- B. To make the bone rub
- C. To reduce movement and pain while awaiting care
- D. To force alignment

**Correct answer:** C. To reduce movement and pain while awaiting care

**Explanation:** Support helps limit painful movement and further injury.

### 5. A person may have a broken leg after a serious fall. Should you make them walk to the car?

- A. Yes, to test the injury
- B. No; keep them still and arrange appropriate emergency help
- C. Only if you pull the leg
- D. Yes, if they are embarrassed

**Correct answer:** B. No; keep them still and arrange appropriate emergency help

**Explanation:** Walking can worsen injury; avoid unnecessary movement.

### 6. What should you do if severe bleeding accompanies an open fracture?

- A. Ignore the bleeding
- B. Push bone back in first
- C. Make the person exercise
- D. Control life-threatening bleeding while avoiding pressure directly on exposed bone, and call for help

**Correct answer:** D. Control life-threatening bleeding while avoiding pressure directly on exposed bone, and call for help

**Explanation:** Life-threatening bleeding must be controlled, while protecting exposed bone.

### 7. When using a splint, what is important?

- A. Force the limb into a normal position
- B. Tie it as tightly as possible
- C. Support the injury without forcing alignment and check circulation where trained
- D. Apply it only after the person walks

**Correct answer:** C. Support the injury without forcing alignment and check circulation where trained

**Explanation:** Splints should not compromise circulation or force the injury into alignment.

### 8. A suspected fracture causes numbness or a cold, pale hand beyond the injury. What should you do?

- A. Seek urgent medical help
- B. Ignore it until tomorrow
- C. Massage hard
- D. Wrap it tightly
